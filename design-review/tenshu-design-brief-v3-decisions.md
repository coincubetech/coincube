# Tenshu design brief — V3 decisions and refinements (2026-10-22)

Robert's decisions after reviewing the V2/V3 canvas (https://claude.ai/artifact/4DkSWQshpyXVu2kVrbneRz). These override earlier briefs where they conflict. Still a design review; no application code changes. Everything below needs source validation before implementation, noted per item.

## 1. Launcher direction: spin-to-face, with the compact catalog as fallback

- **Production direction:** the "V3 · Launcher spin-to-face" flow. Selecting a Cube grows it and turns it to a hidden face that carries the Cube details; Open turns it to a second face that carries PIN entry / passkey; checks follow on that face; the Cube opens.
- **Native tier:** "textured face during the spin, live widgets at rest." The details and PIN panels are ordinary Iced widgets positioned at the face rectangle once the face is square to the camera; during the ~820ms turn the face shows a rasterized snapshot of that panel. The prototype's DOM-on-face rendering is the visual reference for this tier, not a request for live widgets on angled geometry (ruled out: breaks screen readers, focus and text input).
- **Motion spec from the prototype:** 820ms turn, cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1); other Cubes dim to 40% and scale 0.92 in place over 500ms; switching Cubes from the details face reverse-turns the outgoing Cube and starts the incoming one 160ms later; the incoming Cube always opens on details with the PIN cleared; nothing animates continuously; PIN input is live the instant the face appears and submission never waits for the turn.
- **Fallback:** "Launcher 3 · Compact catalog" (V1 row) is the backup presentation with identical metadata, filters and actions. It is used (a) by user preference ("Launcher style: Cubes / List" under This device › About & app preferences), (b) automatically under reduced motion, (c) automatically when the GPU/shader path is unavailable or the native spike measures the turn below the frame budget, and (d) offered at high Cube counts. The catalog is never more than one click away from the floor (a list/floor switch in the Launcher top bar).
- **Validation needed:** Iced 0.14 shader widget depth/compositing and snapshot-to-texture path; frame budget on modest hardware; OS reduced-motion signals on macOS/Windows/Linux; GPU-less detection.

## 2. One open Cube at a time (proposed session model)

- Opening a Cube means unlocking it; the app shows one open Cube. The multi-tab Cube switcher in the top bar is replaced by: the active Cube's name + network, and "All Cubes" (returns to the Launcher; the active Cube stays unlocked until the user locks it or the auto-lock timeout fires). This removes the tab clutter Robert noted.
- **Retained cross-Cube cases, handled inside flows rather than tabs:** (a) sending funds between wallets in different Cubes; (b) splitting a Bitcoin wallet's coins into Bitcoin and Bitcoin Blake2b. Proposed: a "Transfer to another Cube" option in the Transfer flow and a dedicated "Split coins" flow pick the target Cube from the catalog and unlock it in place with its own PIN/passkey for the duration of that flow ("second Cube unlocked for this transfer only"), then relock it. The admission and network checks of the second Cube still run; filters and the Launcher never combine or bypass anything.
- **Validation needed:** current runtime session model (can two Cubes be unlocked in one process; what the Blake2b split flow requires of the source wallet; auto-lock semantics). Until validated, the artboards keep "All Cubes" and drop the tab list; the earlier guarantee "returning to the Launcher does not lock open Cubes" becomes "returning to the Launcher does not lock the active Cube."

## 3. Wallet pages: Send, Receive and transactions

- The consolidated wallet pages (Spark, Vault, Liquid) stay. Send and Receive are the next design job; they are not solved by the Overview artboards.
- **Incumbent to improve (Spark send):** "You send" / "They receive" asset blocks with rail tags (SPARK → LIGHTNING), Destination (invoice or Lightning address), Amount (sats, optional with amount-bearing invoices), then Prepare → fee quote → confirm. Keep that two-step (prepare, then confirm with the quote) in every rail; it is the safety step.
- **Direction for the redesign:** one Send flow per wallet with a rail/asset selector where the wallet has more than one (Liquid: L-BTC or USDt; destination type detected from the pasted string and shown as a tag; Spark: Lightning invoice/address, Spark address, on-chain where supported); amount with unit switch and fiat estimate; Prepare → quote (fee, arrival estimate, what the recipient gets) → Confirm. Receive: per wallet, with the receive method as the first choice (Lightning invoice, Lightning Address, Spark address, on-chain, Liquid address; USDt where applicable), a QR + copy field, and amount/description optional. Keep every existing safeguard (recipient checks, Branta strict-mode privacy note, address verification on hardware signers for Vault).
- **Transactions:** the sidebar's Activity entry becomes the all-wallet transaction list with per-wallet, direction, status and date filters, each row opening a detail view (amounts, fees, confirmations/status, ids with copy, labels per BIP329, rail-specific details under disclosure). Each wallet page shows its own recent rows with "See all in Activity." The current dedicated Transactions page's contents are the content requirement; its layout is not.
- **Validation needed:** exact destination types per rail at baseline; Liquid USDt send/receive constraints; label storage; what detail fields each rail exposes.

## 4. Connect features at the Launcher level

- Connect (currently a sidebar submenu inside a Cube: Overview, Contacts, Plan & Billing, Security) moves to the Launcher level, reached from the account entry in the Launcher top bar and from the account chip in the open-Cube top bar. Proposed Connect page: account identity and sign-in state; Plan & billing (verified status, history, compare plans secondary); Contacts; Devices & security (current device, device review, login activity; Duress gated); Recovery services summary (Recovery Kit and alert eligibility per Cube, linking into that Cube's Backup & protection after unlock). Cube-scoped Connect state (this Cube's service session, registration, entitlements) stays inside the Cube under Settings › Connections.
- **Next deliverable:** a mockup of this Connect page in the Launcher world (requested by Robert; not yet drawn).

## 5. Universal toggles

- Two installation-wide toggles in the top bar of both the Launcher and the open Cube: Hide balances (eye) and Light/Dark theme. They replace the per-section eye toggles on the Overview; a single state applies everywhere. Both are "This device" preferences (About & app preferences remains their settings home; the top bar is the shortcut). Hidden balances render as "••••••" everywhere, including the Launcher catalog where no balances are shown anyway, and transaction rows.
- **Validation needed:** where the current theme and hide-balance preferences persist (per device vs per Cube).

## 6. Overview: V2 compact layout is the model

- Robert prefers "V2 · Compact window (1024×768)" for the Overview: wallet containers with the fiat amount and sats on the header row, asset rows stacking ticker over amount with Send/Receive pills on the row. Carry that density to 1440 (wider containers, same row structure) instead of the 1440 V2 layout. V3's no-card ledger is kept as an explored alternative, not the direction.

## Carry-forward from earlier decisions (unchanged)
Concept C sidebar with drawn icons + words on its own surface; Wallets grouped; Expert group removed in favor of per-Vault Advanced; foot = Settings only; top bar carries account and Cube connection state; cube faces: network on top, wallets left, lock + single backup badge right; outline hue = chain family; aura = selection only.

## 7. Refinements after the Connect mockup (2026-10-22)

- **No "All Cubes › Connect" breadcrumb.** Connect is a layer over the Launcher, not a route beneath it. The panel carries its own heading ("Connect account") and a Close control that returns to the Launcher; the top bar stays the Launcher's top bar with the account entry shown as active.
- **Theme moves to App preferences** as a three-way choice: System (default) / Light / Dark. The header keeps only the Hide balances eye toggle; there is no theme control in any top bar.
- **App preferences page (Launcher level, "This device").** Reached from "App preferences" in the Launcher top bar and from Settings › About & app preferences inside a Cube. Contents: Appearance (System/Light/Dark), Hide balances (the same device-wide state the eye toggle flips), Launcher style (Cubes / List, with the automatic fallback note), Motion (Follow system / Reduced), Amount unit default and fiat estimate defaults only if source confirms they are device-scoped (otherwise shown as Cube-scoped elsewhere), Developer mode (with its actual current effect stated), Bitcoin Blake2b beta (account-gated; state shown, never a network switch), Diagnostics (sanitized logs, repair device registration), About (version, build provenance, daemon/bridge versions under details, Download Stats). Every row names its scope.
- **Bitcoin Blake2b mark.** Robert supplied the logo (https://neoxa.exchange/logos/btcb2.png: a gold coin with a ₿). It replaces the text-only "Blake2b" tag as the network mark; the text tag remains beside it. Asset on the canvas: `/_blob/c39785504daaef5633099a8ae196c784`. The chain-family outline hue for Blake2b becomes gold (from the logo, approx #D4A62A) instead of the stand-in blue.
- **Coin-plus-rail badge pattern is kept for both chains.** The asset mark is the chain coin (orange ₿ disc for Bitcoin; gold Blake2b coin for Bitcoin Blake2b) with a small round badge at the bottom-right naming the rail: Spark (black disc, white spark), Lightning (bolt), Liquid (teal), Vault/mainchain (chain link). Bitcoin Blake2b has or will have the same layer-two rails, so the pattern must work on the gold coin too.
- **Bitcoin mark (2026-10-22).** Robert supplied the standard orange Bitcoin logo (the featured image of https://www.cointribune.com/en/tout-savoir-au-sujet-du-logo-bitcoin-2/, orange disc with the tilted white ₿). It replaces every drawn ₿ used as the Bitcoin network mark or as the BTC asset coin. Asset on the canvas: `/_blob/dbbfc88f16f7cc27510d81f434c268ea`. Bitcoin test networks (Testnet4, Signet, Regtest) render the logo at 60% opacity inside a dashed ring with the explicit network tag, the same treatment as Blake2b Testnet4. The rail badge at the bottom-right (Spark, Lightning, Liquid, Vault) stays on top of the logo disc.
